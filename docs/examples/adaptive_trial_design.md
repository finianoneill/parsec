## The core document

FDA's central document on adaptive trials is not oncology-specific: "Adaptive Design Clinical Trials for 
Drugs and Biologics — Guidance for Industry," dated December 2019 under docket FDA-2018-D-3124. It was 
announced as final guidance in the Federal Register on December 2, 2019 (84 FR 65983–65985) and finalizes 
a prior draft. A secondary summary indicates the draft was issued in 2018 and finalized in December 2019. 
It was issued jointly by CBER and CDER, and a review article notes it contains nonbinding recommendations 
and presents seven major adaptive design elements. Its primary focus is adaptive designs for trials 
intended to support the effectiveness and safety of drugs and biologics.

## What it asks of sponsors

Pre-specification is central: FDA states it expects the details of the adaptive design to be completely 
specified prior to trial initiation and documented accordingly (guidance section VIII.B). A secondary 
summary (CASRAI) characterizes the key themes as a clear, pre-specified adaptation plan, adequate control 
of the overall Type I error rate across all planned adaptations and analyses, and maintenance of trial 
integrity including limits on access to unblinded interim data. Another secondary source states FDA 
outlines four key principles, the first being control of the chance of erroneous conclusions. One blog 
comparison of ICH E20 and FDA guidance suggests FDA's pre-trial documentation expectations include the 
rationale, a detailed adaptation plan (number and timing of analyses, modifications, and decision rule), 
and the roles of bodies such as the DMC or adaptation committee. The same source lists Type I error 
control as an FDA consideration for treatment-arm selection adaptations, including adding or dropping 
arms, dose-ranging, seamless designs, and platform trials.

Information important to FDA's evaluation should be provided at the design stage, though it may be spread 
across different documents. The guidance also advises what to submit for Bayesian adaptive and complex 
simulation-based designs. Special protocol assessments for complex adaptive designs are recommended only 
after extensive prior discussion with FDA, given the 45-day response timeline. Marketing applications 
relying on an adaptive trial should include sufficient information and documentation per section VIII.C. 
FDA's paperwork burden estimate was based on review of INDs, NDAs, BLAs, and supplements using adaptive 
designs. A review article notes that sample size re-estimation based on interim analyses of incomplete 
data may inflate Type I error, whereas re-estimation of nuisance parameters such as variance is treated 
differently, and that adaptive analysis generally involves a suitable treatment-effect estimator plus 
inference about that estimate.

## Oncology-specific guidances

"Master Protocols: Efficient Clinical Trial Design Strategies to Expedite Development of Oncology Drugs 
and Biologics" is dated March 2022 and was announced as final in the Federal Register on March 2, 2022 (87
FR 11722–11724). It addresses trials evaluating more than one investigational drug and/or more than one 
cancer type within a single overall trial structure in adult and pediatric cancers. Master protocols are 
described as using a single infrastructure, design, and protocol, unlike traditional designs testing a 
single drug in a single disease population.

"Expansion Cohorts: Use in First-In-Human Clinical Trials to Expedite Development of Oncology Drugs and 
Biologics" is likewise dated March 2022 and was announced at 87 FR 11720–11722. It sets out FDA's current 
thinking on the characteristics of drug products best suited to development under a multiple expansion 
cohort trial, with the 2022 revision making minor clarifications to those suitability criteria.

A broader draft, "Master Protocols for Drug and Biological Product Development," is dated June 2026 and is
marked "not for implementation" and distributed for comment.
