# Parsec v2 — Enhancement Plan

**Status:** v2.0 draft for review
**Audience:** implementation agent + human owner
**Basis:** v1 complete (M0–M6, 157 tests, byte-identical replay throughout) + a five-track deep-research pass over the 2025–2026 state of the art in deep-research architectures, claim verification, confidence calibration, retrieval infrastructure, and agent evaluation. Sources in Appendix A.

---

## 0. What the research says (and what it means for us)

Five findings reshape the v2 agenda; everything below follows from them.

1. **The field converged on parsec's architecture — and moved past its cut corners.** Orchestrator + context-isolated parallel subagents + single-writer synthesis + a separate citation pass is now the reference shape (Anthropic's multi-agent research system; LangChain Open Deep Research; Tongyi DeepResearch). Claim–evidence graphs are the emerging fix for untrustworthy citations (ADORE, ArbGraph). Frozen-corpus evaluation went from parsec's quirk to published best practice (BrowseComp-Plus, DeepResearchGym). Our bets are validated; our v1 simplifications (sequential subagents, URL-domain dedup, exact-match-only containment, static source tiers, flat volatility penalty) are now the gaps.
2. **Parallelism and byte-identical replay are not in conflict.** The durable-execution world (Temporal, Restate) solved concurrent execution + deterministic replay years ago: record every nondeterministic result *and the completion order* as events, replay by feeding recorded results through a deterministic scheduler. v1's sequential-for-determinism constraint can be lifted without giving up T4.
3. **Mechanical citation checks overstate faithfulness by 20–50 points.** Across 2026 audits of commercial deep-research agents, link validity runs >94% while *factual support* runs 39–77% ("Cited but Not Verified"). v1's structural verification is the necessary tier the field says is insufficient alone; the missing tier — claim-support checking with small grounded-NLI models — now has permissively-licensed local models (HHEM-2.1-Open 110M Apache; FactCG-DeBERTa 0.4B; Granite Guardian Apache) that make it ~free to run constantly, exactly as §8 of the v1 brief hoped.
4. **Noisy-OR over URL domains is the weakest link in the credence model.** Hashing/domain clustering measurably fails on syndicated and paywalled news (NEWS-COPY, NDD-NS benchmarks); correlated sources inflate corroboration (circular reporting). The literature's fixes are all local-friendly: embedding-based syndication clustering (semhash-class, CPU-only), conflict-aware aggregation (subjective-logic opinions instead of pure noisy-OR), learned per-source reliability (truth-discovery iteration over our own evidence graph), and mutability-classified time decay (FreshQA taxonomy) instead of a flat volatile penalty. And calibration is tractable: Platt scaling needs only ~200–1000 labeled claims — a size our eval harness can produce.
5. **Eval gold has converged on many binary rubrics, not single scores; regression stats have converged on paired differences.** DeepResearch Bench II uses 9,430 binary rubrics; TREC-style nugget recall is validated specifically for *version ranking* — our exact regression use case. Anthropic's "Adding Error Bars to Evals" paired-difference analysis is a free variance reduction for our identical-corpora A/B runner.

Also material: search-provider ToS now constrain caching (Brave prohibits persistent storage of *search results* without a storage-rights plan — our self-fetched document archive is fine, provider responses are not); stdlib HTML extraction is measurably far below trafilatura (F1 ~0.94); the robots/402/RSL landscape rewards an identity-honest fetcher; vendor churn (Tavily→Nebius, Jina→Elastic, Bing API retirement) rewards thin disposable adapters — which we already have.

## 1. v2 Design Theses (extending T1–T7)

- **T8 — Concurrency is recorded, not forbidden.** Parallel subagents are allowed; determinism comes from journaling every nondeterministic boundary *and its arrival order*. Replay feeds recorded results through a deterministic scheduler. Sequentialism was a v1 scaffold, not a principle.
- **T9 — Verification is tiered by trust, and the mechanical tier is never enough.** Structure (v1) < grounded-NLI claim support (v2, small local models) < judge (advisory). Every new verifier gets measured on public benchmarks (LLM-AggreFact, Qualifire) before it gates anything.
- **T10 — Credence must survive contact with correlated, conflicting, and stale evidence.** Corroboration counts independent *content clusters*, not domains; conflict lowers confidence rather than averaging away; facts carry mutability classes and can be superseded; and the numbers get recalibrated against measured outcomes or they stay internal.
- **T11 — The archive is ours; provider responses are borrowed.** Two cache regimes: the content-addressed archive of self-fetched documents (permanent, the replay substrate) vs. provider API responses (TTL-bounded, per-provider policy). Fetching is identity-honest: descriptive UA, robots purpose-awareness, 402/RSL surfaced as typed outcomes, never circumvented.
- **T12 — Every improvement lands with its measurement.** No workstream merges without the eval that detects its regression. Paired-difference stats with confidence intervals replace raw threshold gates.

## 2. Workstreams

### WS-A — Retrieval that's real (highest leverage per unit effort)

v1 stubs search; v2 makes parsec usable on the live web without giving up determinism.

1. **Search providers.** Ship `SearXNG` as the default zero-key local provider (fits local-first), plus thin adapters: Brave (raw SERP, cheap, lowest latency), Serper (cheapest Google wrapper), Tavily or Perplexity Search (cleaned content). Extend the `SearchProvider` protocol to three response shapes — raw SERP, cleaned-content chunks, semantic/neural (Exa-style) — rather than one flat hit list. Every provider adapter gets its own record/replay layer keyed like the fetch cache, but **TTL-bounded per provider policy** (T11; Brave ToS).
2. **Extraction upgrade.** Replace stdlib HTMLParser with `trafilatura` (deterministic, pure-Python+lxml, F1 ≈ 0.94 vs ~0.91 readability) with readability-lxml fallback; add a structure-preserving HTML→Markdown pass for tables/code. PDF path: PyMuPDF4LLM as the deterministic default; GROBID (Docker sidecar) for scholarly PDFs feeding the structured adapters. Extractor version already stamps `meta_json` — extraction stays a pure cached function, so ML-based extractors (marker `--use_llm`) are allowed only *behind* the cache.
3. **`search_within` (corpus-internal hybrid search).** SQLite FTS5 (BM25) + `sqlite-vec` brute-force KNN (exact = deterministic) + reciprocal-rank fusion, all in the existing DB file. Embeddings computed once per (model-hash, text-hash), stored content-addressed — embedding becomes a cacheable pure function. Default model: nomic-embed-text-v1.5 or EmbeddingGemma (CPU/ONNX int8 for reproducibility). Every deep-research architecture in the survey re-queries already-gathered evidence; our citable, replayable spans make ours better.
4. **Structured adapters** (priority order by agent-friendliness): SEC EDGAR (free, JSON, strict UA rules), Crossref/OpenAlex (DOI resolution; OpenAlex now key-gated with credits), arXiv (fragile — 1 req/3s, cache hard, prefer bulk S3), Semantic Scholar. Adapters are first-class citizens of the politeness subsystem: per-API quota budgets, mandatory identification headers, backoff.
5. **Politeness 2.0.** robots.txt parsing with purpose-awareness, HTTP 402 and RSL license terms recognized and surfaced as typed fetch outcomes ("licensed content" is a result, not an error), Wayback CDX + Common Crawl adapters as dead-link/provenance fallbacks. Record per fetch: robots decision, final URL, timestamp, content hash — a replayed run can prove what was retrieved and under what terms. No paywall circumvention, ever.

### WS-B — Evals 2.0 (measurement before tuning)

1. **Nugget-based gold.** Evolve `must_find` substring lists into weighted binary rubric items ("nuggets", vital/okay), LLM-drafted from the case's gold documents and human-edited, graded support/partial/none. Keep substring/regex as the strict mechanical tier. Add key-point *contradiction* checks (report contradicts a gold nugget → penalty).
2. **Hard negatives in cases.** Frozen corpora gain deliberately-mined distractor documents (BrowseComp-Plus pattern) so retrieval skill is actually stressed, plus per-case verified supporting-doc lists.
3. **Claim-support scoring axis.** New mechanical-ish axis between citation-structure and judge: grounded-NLI verification of each claim against its cited spans *from the frozen fetch cache* — fully reproducible support checking, which no live-web evaluation can offer. Report ALCE-style citation precision/recall with full/partial/none grades, and track faithfulness as a function of trajectory length (documented degradation mode).
4. **Statistics.** The regression runner reports **paired per-case deltas with confidence intervals** (Anthropic error-bars methodology), three-state verdicts per axis (improved/regressed/inconclusive), N=3–5 runs per case with early stopping on clearly-unchanged cases. Mechanical axes gate every merge; judge axes run nightly with multi-run averaging.
5. **Judge upgrades.** Reference-relative scoring (the load-bearing ingredient per DeepResearch Bench ablations), per-case generated binary criteria instead of one synthesis score, position-swapped pairwise mode for A/B regression, and if a panel: two genuinely different model families beats N same-family judges (correlated-error result).
6. **Trajectory metrics** (nearly free given the event log): searches-to-first-gold-hit, gold-doc fetch fraction, redundant-query rate, loop detection, recovery-after-failed-fetch, tokens/cost per case — reported in the regression diff so efficiency regressions surface even when scores hold.

### WS-C — Verification depth (closing the v1 cut list, with better tools than v1 planned)

1. **Grounded-NLI claim support (the deferred "NLI-lite," upgraded).** Not vanilla DeBERTa-MNLI — the field moved to grounded factual-consistency checkers trained on synthetic errors. Two-tier local stack: HHEM-2.1-Open (110M, Apache) or FactCG-DeBERTa (0.4B) as the always-on pass; Granite Guardian 3.3-8B (Apache) as escalation. **License trap:** Bespoke-MiniCheck-7B tops the leaderboard but is CC BY-NC — excluded by default. Runs at `record_premises` time (advisory warning to the subagent) and in verification stage 2 (recorded verdict). MiniCheck-class models verify at sentence level without decomposition — so we check whole premises and decompose only on failure.
2. **Span-level unsupported-content detection.** LettuceDetect-class models (MIT, ModernBERT, 150–610M) output *which spans* are unsupported — the inverse of our containment check, and it maps directly onto our span-addressed evidence. Candidate for flagging exactly what part of a premise overreaches.
3. **Claim quality lints (anti-"DAG theater," §10.1 of the v1 brief).** Adopt Claimify's discipline: an explicit **ambiguity-refusal gate** (premises that can't be decontextualized are rejected with the reason, not recorded vaguely), a decontextualization lint (does the claim carry its referents?), and verifier-aware granularity (don't over-fragment; "molecular" not atomic). Score our own decomposition quality with DecMetrics-style metrics in the eval suite.
4. **Temporal validator (the deferred `temporal` edge check).** Blueprint from ChronoFact: normalize timestamps on evidence (publication date + in-text time expressions), build event timelines, check ordering claims by Allen-interval-style constraint satisfaction — mechanical once events are timestamped, no LLM in the loop. TSVer + Qualifire Time&Dates are the eval sets. Nobody ships a mechanical temporal validator today; this is a genuine differentiator.
5. **Dual-perspective retrieval for conflict discovery.** Cheap mechanical trick from the literature: also search for the *negation* of high-stakes claims; hits feed `contradicts` edges instead of hoping conflicts surface by accident.

### WS-D — Credence 2.0 + calibration

1. **Independence via content, not domains.** Replace URL-domain corroboration clusters with embedding-based syndication clustering (semhash-class static embeddings, CPU-only, MIT; MinHash only as a prefilter — hashing measurably fails on news). Corroboration discounts by estimated shared ancestry (near-dup cluster membership, shared-quote overlap), fixing circular-reporting inflation.
2. **Conflict-aware aggregation.** Move from pure noisy-OR to subjective-logic opinions (belief / disbelief / uncertainty triples): degrades to noisy-OR when evidence is independent, but natively represents "two strong sources disagree" (which must *lower* confidence — v1 cannot express this) and "no evidence" vs "conflicting evidence." `contradicts` edges finally participate in propagation instead of being display-only.
3. **Learned source reliability.** Replace the hand-rolled static tier table's core with: (a) open aggregated domain-quality ratings (Lin et al.'s 11.5k-domain "wisdom of experts" set, CRED-1) as the free baseline prior, (b) optional MBFC/NewsGuard API plug-ins, (c) truth-discovery iteration that updates per-source reliability from agreement patterns already present in our own evidence graph. Priors stay priors — overridable, provenance-stamped ("prior from X, adjusted by observed agreement").
4. **Mutability-classified decay + supersession.** Replace the flat volatile penalty with FreshQA-style classes (never/slow/fast-changing) and age-dependent decay computed from *evidence timestamps* (still clock-free at verification time: age = report time − evidence time, both recorded). Newer evidence contradicting older evidence on a mutable claim **supersedes** rather than averages.
5. **`parsec calibrate`.** The v1 brief's promise, now concrete: log every rendered credence; label outcomes from eval corpora (judge-assisted + human spot-checks); fit Platt/temperature scaling (NOT isotonic below ~1000 points); report Brier score, smooth ECE, and a risk-coverage curve. UX after calibration: tiers backed by quantified *ranges* on expansion ("60–75%", never "0.68") — ranges don't damage trust, unquantified hedges do (van der Bles). Per-claim uncertainty *provenance* ("single source" / "conflicting sources" / "possibly stale") — the graph produces it directly, and it's what guides user verification effort.

### WS-E — Parallel subagents with deterministic replay

The Temporal pattern, adapted to our event log:

1. **Per-subagent event streams.** Each subagent gets its own ordered stream (`session_id`, `stream_id`, `stream_idx`); internally sequential and deterministic exactly as today. The orchestrator's stream records **join events** capturing the completion/merge order actually observed. Replay = replay each stream against its recorded results + replay the recorded join order through a deterministic scheduler. The projection comparison extends per-stream + join-order.
2. **Journaled boundaries.** Already true for LLM calls and fetches; extend to any new nondeterminism (embedding calls, provider queries). Restate's trap to avoid: unconstrained parallel tool calls inside one logical step — parallel fan-out happens only at the orchestrator's dispatch boundary.
3. **Scope: parallelize research, never writing.** LangChain's negative result (parallel section-writers produce disjoint reports) confirms our single-writer design. Parallelism cap N≤5 per §3 of the v1 brief; budget ledger attribution per stream (already per-actor).
4. **Failure semantics.** Anthropic's named challenges — result coordination, state consistency, error propagation — become the test list: a subagent dying mid-wave must leave a resolvable coverage row and a replayable stream.

### WS-F — Orchestration & context polish

1. **Scoping phase.** A distinct clarify→brief step before planning (every major product converged on this): the decomposer produces a persisted *research brief* artifact; with steering, the brief becomes an approvable/editable gate (our steering events already make this replay-safe — a differentiator, since products treat steering as untracked UI state).
2. **Round-based context reconstruction.** The field moved past transcript compaction to workspace reconstruction (Tongyi IterResearch, Manus todo-recitation, Anthropic artifact-based state). Our evidence DAG *is* the external memory: compaction rung 2 becomes "re-render the relevant DAG slice + notebook" — a deterministic function of the log, replacing the deferred model-written squeeze with something better and cheaper.
3. **KV-cache-conscious prompt layout.** Stable prefixes and append-only suffixes are already our shape (§7); audit new prompt assembly (premise lists, briefs) to keep prefix-stability under cache pricing.
4. **Spend-aware effort scaling.** Anthropic's finding: token spend explains ~80% of performance variance; scale subagent count/turn budgets to query complexity (simple = 1 subagent few calls; complex = full fan-out) — the decomposer emits an effort estimate the budget ledger enforces.

## 3. Milestones

Each ends runnable, tested, and measured (T12). **v2 definition of done: M7–M10.** M11–M12 are high-value stretch.

- **M7 — Live retrieval.** SearXNG + Brave/Serper adapters behind the extended protocol; TTL-bounded provider caches vs. permanent self-fetch archive; trafilatura extraction (+markdown pass); `search_within` (FTS5 + sqlite-vec + RRF, cached embeddings); politeness 2.0 (robots purposes, 402/RSL typed outcomes, identity UA). *Exit test: a live query end-to-end with a real provider, then replayed byte-identically offline; a 402/RSL-gated URL surfaces as a typed licensed-content outcome; extraction quality regression-tested on a fixture set.*
- **M8 — Evals 2.0.** Nugget rubrics with vital/okay weights + contradiction checks; hard negatives in case corpora; claim-support axis scored against the frozen cache with a grounded-NLI model; paired-difference regression stats with CIs and three-state verdicts; trajectory metrics in the diff. *Exit test: a deliberately-degraded harness variant is flagged "regressed" with correct CIs on the right axis, an unchanged variant reads "inconclusive/no change," and a case with hard negatives separates a good retriever from a lucky one.*
- **M9 — Verification depth.** Grounded-NLI premise checking (two-tier local stack, benchmarked on LLM-AggreFact/Qualifire before gating anything); span-level unsupported-content flags; Claimify-style ambiguity-refusal + decontextualization lints; mechanical temporal validator; dual-perspective conflict retrieval. *Exit test: a paraphrased-but-unsupported premise that passes v1 exact-match containment is caught by the NLI tier; an ordering claim contradicted by evidence timestamps is mechanically flagged; a vague premise ("the study showed benefits") is refused at record time with the reason.*
- **M10 — Credence 2.0 + calibration.** Embedding-based syndication clustering; subjective-logic aggregation with conflict lowering confidence; learned source reliability over open rating baselines; mutability classes + supersession; `parsec calibrate` with Brier/ECE/risk-coverage and range-backed tier rendering. *Exit test: two syndicated copies of one wire story corroborate no more than one does; two strong disagreeing sources yield lower credence than either alone; calibration on ≥200 labeled claims measurably improves Brier vs. the uncalibrated heuristic; superseded stale facts render as superseded, not averaged.*
- **M11 — Deterministic parallelism.** Per-subagent event streams, recorded join order, deterministic replay scheduler, N≤5 concurrent subagents, per-stream budget attribution, failure-semantics test suite. *Exit test: a 3-subagent concurrent run replays byte-identically per-stream and in join order; killing one subagent mid-wave leaves a blocked-with-reason ledger row and a replayable session.*
- **M12 — Orchestration polish.** Research-brief scoping phase with approvable gate; DAG-slice context reconstruction replacing compaction rung 2; effort-scaled dispatch; KV-cache prefix audit. *Exit test: a long subagent run reconstructs context from the DAG and completes with identical evidence to the unreconstructed run; brief-gate approval and edits are steering events that replay.*

## 4. Risks & honest uncertainty

1. **NLI error rates are real.** Paraphrase and negation remain the classic failure axes even for grounded checkers; per-atom confidence discrimination is documented as weak. Mitigation: NLI never sole-gates high-stakes claims (T9); measure each model on public benchmarks before trusting it; keep exact-match as the floor.
2. **Model licenses are a minefield.** The leaderboard-topping checker is non-commercial. Default stack is Apache/MIT-only; any NC model is opt-in with a loud config flag.
3. **Learned source reliability can self-reinforce.** Truth-discovery iteration on a small personal corpus may amplify early errors. Mitigation: heavy prior anchoring to the open rating baselines, per-domain provenance display, and a cap on learned adjustment magnitude until calibration data justifies more.
4. **Calibration labels are expensive and biased.** Judge-assisted labeling inherits judge biases. Mitigation: human spot-check quota, cross-family judges, and reporting calibration quality (Brier/ECE) rather than asserting it.
5. **Deterministic concurrency is subtle.** The failure mode is a replay that diverges only under rare interleavings. Mitigation: property-style tests over randomized (recorded-seed) schedules; keep the sequential mode as a config fallback forever.
6. **Provider ToS drift.** Caching policies (Brave), pricing (Exa/OpenAlex), and acquisitions (Tavily) change under us. Mitigation: adapters stay thin and disposable (validated by v1); per-provider cache policy is config, not code.
7. **Scope (again, the biggest).** Six workstreams is v1's §10.7 all over again. The cut lines below are the enforcement mechanism.

**What to cut if forced to halve v2:** Exa/semantic search shape, GROBID sidecar, Wayback/CC adapters, learned source reliability (keep open-dataset priors only), subjective logic (keep noisy-OR + a conflict penalty), M12 entirely. **What survives any cut:** trafilatura, one real search provider + TTL cache split, `search_within`, the claim-support eval axis, paired-difference stats, grounded-NLI premise checking, embedding-based syndication clustering, `parsec calibrate`.
